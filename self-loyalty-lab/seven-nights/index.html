Seven nights. One promise. Kept.

Pick one small rule for yourself. Keep it for seven nights. On the seventh night you will have something you have never had, which is your own record instead of your own mood.

You have made the promise before. "I will not text him first." "I will not check his stories tonight." And by 11:40, you did.

That is not because you are weak. It is because the promise was too big, it was made in a calm moment, and nobody helped you keep it on the night it got hard.

Before you start

Where should I send your reminders?

Seven nights is easier when someone nudges you each evening. Enter your email and I will send one short reminder a night, and nothing else you did not ask for. Unsubscribe any time.

Only your email is sent, so I can reach you during the week. Your nightly lines stay on your device.

Step one

Pick one rule

Small enough to keep. Real enough to matter. Most people fail because the rule was too big, not because they are.

How do I know if my rule is small enough?

Too big: "I will not think about him." You cannot keep a rule about a feeling. "I will go no contact for a month." That is four rules and a deadline.

Just right: "I do not reply after 10 PM." One behavior, one boundary, and you know by bedtime whether you kept it.

If you are not sure, pick the smaller one. A kept small rule teaches you more than a broken big one.

Step two, about ten minutes

Write your plan for the hard moment

The moment you will need this is the moment your judgment is worst. So it gets decided now, in daylight, by the version of you who can think.

Open your 2AM Card. Three if-then lines and the name of one person you reach out to instead of him. Then keep it where you will find it.

This kind of if-then plan is one of the most tested tools in behavior research. It reliably helps people follow through on things they already wanted to do.
